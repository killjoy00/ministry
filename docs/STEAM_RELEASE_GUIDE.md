# From playable prototype to Steam: the Ministry of Coincidences

**Prepared November 8, 2026.** This project is a one-case offline pre-alpha, **not yet a commercial-release-ready game** or a Valve Deck Verified title. This guide separates the steps an owner must personally complete from the steps our automated builds can support.

## What the owner does in Steamworks

1. **Enroll as a Steamworks partner:** https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect. Use the Steam account that will own the publishing relationship. Accept the NDA and distribution agreement. Enter your exact legal individual/business name; a sole proprietor may apply under their own legal name. Bank-account ownership must match the legal entity. Complete tax and identity verification. This is private information: do not put it in GitHub or share it with an AI agent.
2. **Pay the Steam Direct fee:** $100 USD per app (and possible applicable taxes). The charge is non-refundable but recoupable after $1,000 in adjusted gross revenue from the app. Activate the purchased app credit to create the game's Steam app and record its **App ID**. Valve's landing page currently says a 21-day wait before the first release while parts of the detailed onboarding documentation still state 30 days; plan for up to 30 days and check the actual Steamworks account eligibility/status. Start onboarding now, even if you won't launch soon.
3. **Prepare the app's store presence:** In Steamworks open the new app, complete the Store Presence checklist, content descriptors, category, platform support, languages, developer/publisher attribution and pricing. We recommend initial tags such as *Strategy*, *Roguelite*, *Simulation*, *Casual*, *Indie* and *Choices Matter* only if the final gameplay supports them. Use bespoke capsule/key art and **real gameplay screenshots from the current build**, not staged illustrations masquerading as screenshots.
4. **Declare AI-assisted content in the Content Survey:** The shipped portraits, Oddford panorama, event illustrations and stamp art are *pre-generated AI-assisted images*. The offline event engine does not make live generative-AI calls. Proposed accurate disclosure: “Pre-generated, AI-assisted illustrations used for character portraits, environments, incidents, and bureaucratic stamp icons. All assets are reviewed and packaged before release. Gameplay uses a deterministic offline event simulation; no content is generated by AI during play.” Validate rights/consistency and any other AI-assisted content you later add.
5. **Submit the store page for review:** Finish the checklist and select *Mark as ready for review* in Steamworks. Store review typically takes **3–5 business days**; budget at least a week for edits. After approval, publish a **Coming Soon** page; it must be visible for **at least two weeks** before the game can launch, during which players can wishlist it. Publishing Coming Soon does **not** release the game.

## What the repository/automation can support

6. **Build and configure Steam depots:** Our `.github/workflows/builds.yml` exports native Linux and Windows `.exe`/`.x86_64` plus the corresponding `.pck`. After App ID and depot IDs exist, configure a SteamPipe upload using the official Steamworks SDK, with a Windows depot and a Linux depot; ensure the Linux executable has executable permissions. In Steamworks > General Installation Settings, set the launch options for Windows `ministry-of-coincidences-windows.exe` and Linux `ministry-of-coincidences-linux.x86_64`, limited to their respective OS. SteamPipe build/Steam Guard credentials must be configured through a secure dedicated publishing account, **not committed to GitHub**. A first upload still requires Steamworks permissions and possible interactive Steam Guard authorization.
7. **Run a private Steam test first:** Steamworks offers private test branches and **Steam Playtest**, which associates a separate child App ID with your main game's store listing and allows controlled tester access. Use this before public release. Test controller navigation, readable text at 1280×800, Steam Deck launch, save/quit/relaunch, suspend/resume, and behavior across the seven-day case. Resolve game-breaking issues and expand the content before charging money.
8. **Submit the build for Valve review:** After the store page has been submitted, upload a mostly final build and complete the Build checklist. Click *Mark as ready for review*. The build must genuinely deliver the features promised on the store page. Review/approval and any requested fixes happen before release.
9. **Select a launch window and release deliberately:** Verify that your fee wait, tax/identity, 14-day Coming Soon window, approved store page, reviewed build and pricing are all satisfied. When you're ready, use Steamworks' **Release App**, then confirm **Publish Now / Release Now**. Nothing is automatically released on its own. The account needs the Steamworks app permissions to publish and manage pricing.
10. **After launch:** Keep updating builds using the same SteamPipe depots and branches, monitor crash reports/player feedback, and revalidate Steam Deck compatibility. A native Linux build can run on Deck without a Verified badge; only Valve can award the official compatibility rating.

## Immediate next actions and owner dependencies

- [ ] Personally complete Steamworks onboarding and pay the app fee; keep banking and tax information private.
- [ ] Create the Steam app and supply only the **public/nonsecret App ID** and depot IDs when available.
- [ ] Confirm whether this first Steam presence is planned as **Coming Soon + Steam Playtest** (recommended), a demo, or a full paid launch.
- [ ] Independently test the Linux build on real Steam Deck hardware, or recruit someone to perform this task.
- [ ] Build full-game content: multiple cases, stronger incremental progression and polish. The single-case pre-alpha is **not** ready to sell as the finished game.
